## Why this order

The design's option 6a removes the Explore view, moves seeds into the
rail and replaces the focus projection with an additive Scope model
(collections stay visible, hops add neighbours, rows filter by
checkbox). That is the answer to D1 and makes D2 moot, so neither is
fixed on the current focus model. B4's labels depend on which toolbar
entries survive, so they are decided inside the design brainstorm. B6
is fixed before hop fetching is built on the same seed pipeline.

      `Hop 3 1,557/1,557 of 2,726` over `expanding · 627 left · Stop`, and
      the three numbers are in two units: 1,557 and 2,726 count papers, 627
      counts parents still queued to expand. Of the three, `shown/available`
      is a tautology while the fill runs (what is reached is what is drawn),
      `of {reported}` climbs as each expanded parent adds what it claims
      exists, so it looks like a target that runs away, and only the parent
      count converges. Nothing labels which is which. The spec's reasons are
      sound one at a time (the reported count exists so `50/50 of 1,200`
      never reads as complete; the percentage bar and the `≈ {estimate}` were

      competing predictions. This is a design question, not a defect: the
      code does what `2026-09-12-citation-hops-design.md` specifies. Wants a
      brainstorm on what a reader actually needs during a fill — probably one
      number that converges plus a plain statement of what is still unknown.
      The Stage 3 progress-line check is left open against it.
      The user proposed an answer on 2026-09-13, after watching a fast fill
      appear, count down and disappear cleanly: "We could have a progress
      bar too, would look nicer." Worth weighing against the spec, which
      dropped exactly that — the percentage bar went because a lazy fill
      cannot know a hop's size before its parents are expanded, so a bar

      runs away. A bar over the parent count, which is the only figure that
      converges, is the version the spec's reasoning does not rule out; that

- [ ] D8 the fill fetches far more than the reader needs, and the cost lands
      on the whole plugin. Raised by the user 2026-09-13, mid-walk, after
      watching a hop-3 fill crawl: "This raises a real issue with the user
      experience of this tool. We need more ways to minimize the number of
      fetchs. The whole plugin also get's slower when the fetch is happening
      in large numbers. Hop 3 so far seems to be a hard limit, it would take
      another 2 hours just to start Hop 4." Three claims, and the third is
      the one that bites: a depth the rail offers to 6 is in practice capped
      at 3 by wall clock, so the deep-hop behaviour the design is built
      around is unreachable on a real library. The second claim was narrowed
      by walking the item-tree check against it the same day: Zotero's own
      panes stay responsive, "only the plot lags", so the cost is the
      graph's own rebuild and redraw per landing and the column coalescing
      is doing its job. Distinct from B50, which is the raw rate; this
      is whether a breadth-first walk of every parent is the right shape at
      all. Things a brainstorm should weigh: expanding only what the reader
      has looked at or asked for, expanding the most-cited parents first and
      stopping, a budget the reader sets, reusing what the store already
      holds before any request, and whether hops past 3 should be offered at
      all until this is answered. Stage 4 builds on the fill, so this wants
      settling before Stage 4's spec, not after. A second reason the deep
      hops are not worth their cost, from the same walk: in the citers
      direction the chain runs out against the present. "I can go as far as
      Hop 6 by skipping the other Hops, but there are really no Hop 6 items.
      Hop 5 is already 2026, so in this case there are no new items yet."
      Each hop of citers is roughly a step forward in time, so a seed of any
      age reaches the current year within a few hops and the rows past it are
      structurally empty, not merely unfetched. That is a reason to derive
      the depth the rail offers from the data rather than fix it at 6, and it
      also means a row reading `not fetched` and a row that can never have
      anything are indistinguishable today

- [ ] D11 a reference fill lands a lot of papers with no readable metadata.
      Raised by the user 2026-09-13 walking the Cornerstones check: "The
      references have a lot of Unknowns. We should investigate a way to
      resolve these unknowns." References are the direction where a provider
      most often returns an identifier and little else, so the hop lands a
      paper the reader cannot judge — no author, no year, which also parks it
      in No data (see B48) and makes it worthless on both axes. To
      investigate before designing: how many of the Unknowns carry a DOI or
      other identifier a second lookup would resolve, whether the
      membership-first fetch drops metadata the provider did return, and
      whether unresolved papers should be drawn at all or held until they
      resolve. Nothing is measured yet. Mechanism, from the spec, worked out
      with the user on 2026-09-13 when they asked "How do we know we have
      unknowns? And if we know we have unkowns, we should be able to at least
      know the author and title, or am I missing something here?": an
      expansion runs "membership first, summaries hydrated cooperatively", so
      the edge list (identifiers) lands first and the title, author and year
      arrive later in idle slices. An Unknown is therefore a paper we know
      exists by identifier whose summary has not hydrated or whose hydration
      failed — and the user is right that the identifier is what a second
      lookup would resolve. First hypothesis to test: with the fill crawling
      at one expansion every fifteen seconds (B50), the cooperative
      hydration is starved behind the membership requests, so the Unknowns
      are a symptom of the rate and not of missing data. Worked examples the
      user pulled on 2026-09-13, which point at a second cause: the paper
      `10.1145/1201775.882296` has no year of its own, and two of its
      references, `10.5555/839277.840020` and `10.5555/826029.826529`, come
      back "DOI Not Found". Both failures share the `10.5555` prefix, which
      is what legacy ACM and DBLP proceedings records carry and is frequently
      not registered with the resolver — so this class of Unknown is not a
      paper we failed to fetch but a reference whose DOI was never
      resolvable. Check that before anything else: if it holds, the fix is a
      fallback (title and author lookup, or the provider's own identifier
      rather than the DOI) and not more fetching. The parent having no year
      is B48's territory
